You may have heard that a day on Venus lasts longer than its year. It is a memorable fact, but one word is doing a lot of work: **day**.

Venus takes about 243 Earth days to rotate once relative to distant stars, while a trip around the Sun takes about 225 Earth days. Its rotation period is longer than its orbital period. A sunrise-to-sunrise cycle is different: that takes about 117 Earth days. Venus also turns in the opposite direction from Earth. [NASA explains the rotation and orbit](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/all-about-venus/en/).

The distinction matters when you turn the fact into a quiz. Asking whether “a day is longer than a year” makes the reader guess which kind of day the writer means. Asking about **one complete rotation** gives everyone the same question.

That small edit is part of the Quiznauts approach. Keep the surprise; make the wording fair. A precise question can be more interesting than a dramatic one because it gives you a useful distinction to take away.

It is also a good habit beyond trivia. When a statistic sounds impossible, check what is being measured. Sometimes the explanation lives inside a definition.
